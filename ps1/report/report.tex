\section{Current with a Lorentzian Density of States}

\subsection{Alternative Form}

Given the following expressions for the number of channel electrons and the current in a nanodevice:
\begin{equation}
    N = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}
    \frac{\gamma_1 f_1(E) + \gamma_2 f_2(E)}{\gamma_1 + \gamma_2}
    D(E-U)\, \dd{E}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
    I = \frac{q}{\hbar}
    \frac{\gamma_1 \gamma_2}{\gamma_1 + \gamma_2}
    \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}
    [f_1(E) - f_2(E)] D(E-U)\, \dd{E}
\end{equation}

These expressions can be rewritten into a form that is more convenient for numerical implementation in MATLAB. First, define a change of variables:
\begin{align*}
	E' &= E - U \\
	E &= E' + U \\
	\dd{E'} &= \dd{E}
\end{align*}

Since subtracting a constant does not alter the limits of integration, the integration bounds remain:
\[
    E' : -\infty \rightarrow \infty
\]

Substituting $E = E' + U$ and relabeling $E' \rightarrow E$ yields:
\begin{equation}
    N = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}
    \frac{\gamma_1 f_1(E+U) + \gamma_2 f_2(E+U)}{\gamma_1 + \gamma_2}
    D(E)\, \dd{E}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
    I = \frac{q}{\hbar}
    \frac{\gamma_1 \gamma_2}{\gamma_1 + \gamma_2}
    \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}
    [f_1(E+U) - f_2(E+U)] D(E)\, \dd{E}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Difference Proportional to Current}

By plotting the difference between the Fermi functions using a discrete energy level (modeled as an impulse) rather than a broadened Lorentzian density of states, the relationship between the current $I$ and the difference in the source and drain Fermi functions becomes clearer.

\begin{figure}[H]
	\centering
	\includegraphics[width=10cm]{imgs/fermi_diff_impulse_plot.png}
	\caption{Difference between the Fermi functions as a function of energy, with the density of states modeled as an impulse.}
	\label{fig:fermi_diff_plot}
\end{figure}

\begin{table}[H]
    \centering
    \begin{tabular}{|c|c|c|}
    \hline
    $V_D$ (\unit{\volt}) &
    $\left[f_1(E+U) - f_2(E+U)\right]_{E=\varepsilon}$ &
    $I$ (\unit{\ampere}) \\
    \hline
    0.2 & 0.015 & $1.69 \times 10^{-8}$ \\
    \hline
    0.5 & 0.440 & $2.705 \times 10^{-7}$ \\
    \hline
    0.8 & 0.970 & $5.736 \times 10^{-7}$ \\
    \hline
    1.0 & 1.000 & $6.030 \times 10^{-7}$ \\
    \hline
    \end{tabular}
    \caption{Comparison of the Fermi-function difference and current values at identical drain voltages, evaluated at $\varepsilon = \SI{0.2}{\eV}$.}
    \label{table:sample}
\end{table}

\noindent The ratio of $\left[f_1(E+U) - f_2(E+U)\right]_{E=\varepsilon}$ at $V_D = 1.0$ to that at $V_D = 0.8$, and the ratio at $V_D = 0.8$ to that at $V_D = 0.5$, are found to be $1.031$ and $2.205$, respectively.

\noindent Similarly, the corresponding current ratios are $1.051$ and $2.12$. These results confirm that the current scales proportionally with the difference in the Fermi functions.

\begin{figure}[H]
	\centering
	\includegraphics[width=5cm]{imgs/1c_f1_step.png}
	\includegraphics[width=5cm]{imgs/1c_f2_step.png}
	\caption{Step locations of the Fermi functions at $V_D = \SI{1.0}{\volt}$.}
	\label{fig:1c_fermi_step}
\end{figure}

\noindent At $V_D = \SI{1.0}{\volt}$, the step locations of the Fermi functions were determined to be $\mu_1 = 0.3760$ and $\mu_2 = 0.6240$. Using these values in \verb|problem1c3.py| yields a self-consistent potential of $U = \SI{-0.25027}{\eV}$.

\noindent At this drain voltage, the source contact ($f_1$) favors electron injection into the channel, while the drain contact ($f_2$) favors electron absorbtion from the channel. This can be visualized in Figure \ref{fig:1c_fermi_step} where the Fermi function of $f_1$ steps down at a higher energy than $f_2$. These two biases produce current flow through the channel. Therefore, energies that $\varepsilon$ can be located must be less than $\mu_1 = 0.3760$ and greater than $\mu_2 = 0.6240$.\\

\noindent When $V_D = 0$, the electrochemical potentials of the source and drain are equal ($\mu_1 = \mu_2$), which implies that their Fermi functions are identical ($f_1(E) = f_2(E)$). Consequently, the source is trying to fill (or empty) the channel level to the exact same extent that the drain is. The lack of a difference in agenda leads to no net movement of electrons, and thus the current is zero.
